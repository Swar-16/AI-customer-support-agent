# Billing Dispute Policy

**Effective Date:** 2026-08-28  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Policy Owner:** Billing Operations & Customer Experience  
**Scope:** All standard consumer and business accounts, subscriptions, invoices, payment methods, and charges processed by Company ("Company," "we," "us," or "our").

## 1. General Overview & Eligibility Criteria

### 1.1 Purpose

This policy governs disputes concerning charges appearing on a Company account, including:

- Duplicate charges
- Incorrect billing amounts
- Charges for canceled services
- Charges after a subscription cancellation
- Unauthorized or unrecognized charges
- Incorrect taxes, credits, discounts, or promotional adjustments
- Invoice calculation discrepancies
- Incorrect usage-based charges
- Failed-payment or retry-related billing errors

A **billing dispute** is a customer-initiated request asking Company to investigate, correct, reverse, credit, or otherwise explain a charge.

### 1.2 Eligibility

A customer may submit a billing dispute when all of the following apply:

1. The disputed charge can be reasonably identified by invoice number, transaction ID, payment date, amount, or account information.
2. The customer has an active or recently closed Company account associated with the disputed transaction.
3. The dispute is submitted within the applicable dispute window described below.
4. The customer provides sufficient information for Company to investigate the transaction.
5. The request is not a duplicate of a previously resolved dispute unless materially new evidence is provided.

### 1.3 Standard Dispute Windows

| Dispute Type | Customer Submission Window | Required Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Incorrect amount | **30 calendar days** from invoice/charge date | Invoice or transaction details |
| Duplicate charge | **30 calendar days** | Dates, amounts, or transaction IDs |
| Charge after cancellation | **30 calendar days** from charge date | Cancellation confirmation when available |
| Promotional/discount error | **30 calendar days** | Promotion or offer details |
| Usage/invoice calculation dispute | **30 calendar days** from invoice date | Invoice and disputed line item |
| Unrecognized charge | **60 calendar days** from transaction date | Transaction details and account verification |
| Suspected payment fraud | **60 calendar days** from transaction date | Transaction details; additional identity verification may apply |
| Enterprise contractual dispute | **As specified in the applicable agreement** | Contract/invoice references |

> **Important:** Submission of a dispute does not automatically mean the charge will be refunded. Company investigates the claim and applies the rules applicable to the transaction.

### 1.4 Minimum Prerequisites

Customers should provide:

- Account email or customer ID
- Invoice number, when available
- Transaction date
- Amount and currency
- Last four digits of the payment method, when relevant
- A concise explanation of the disputed charge
- Supporting documentation or screenshots, when available

Customers should **not** provide full payment-card numbers, CVV/security codes, passwords, authentication codes, or other sensitive credentials through support channels.

### 1.5 Standard of Review

Company reviews billing disputes using available account, invoice, payment processor, subscription, usage, cancellation, refund, and communication records.

Depending on the dispute, Company may verify:

- Account ownership
- Subscription status
- Cancellation timestamp
- Invoice generation timestamp
- Payment authorization status
- Payment processor records
- Applied credits or discounts
- Usage records
- Tax calculations
- Prior refunds or dispute outcomes
- Fraud or abuse indicators

## 2. Step-by-Step Operating Procedures

### 2.1 Customer Submission Procedure

1. **Identify the charge.** Locate the invoice, receipt, transaction, or account statement.
2. **Confirm the dispute type.** Classify the issue as an incorrect amount, duplicate charge, cancellation-related charge, unauthorized transaction, or another applicable category.
3. **Submit the dispute.** Use the designated billing-support channel or account dispute workflow.
4. **Provide supporting details.** Include the invoice number or transaction ID, amount, currency, transaction date, and explanation.
5. **Complete verification.** Company may require account authentication or additional identity verification before discussing account-specific billing information.
6. **Retain the case number.** Company assigns a dispute case number for tracking.
7. **Respond to requests for evidence.** Requested information should normally be supplied within **7 calendar days** to avoid administrative closure.
8. **Review the decision.** Company communicates the investigation result and, where applicable, the approved adjustment or refund.
9. **Escalate when appropriate.** A customer may request a secondary review where the initial determination is disputed and new or clarifying evidence exists.

### 2.2 Agent Intake Procedure

Customer support or billing agents must:

1. Verify the customer's authority to access the account.
2. Confirm the disputed transaction and applicable submission window.
3. Categorize the dispute using the billing-dispute reason code.
4. Check for prior disputes, refunds, chargebacks, cancellations, and account credits.
5. Collect required evidence without requesting prohibited payment credentials.
6. Open a dispute case and record the transaction identifier.
7. Apply the applicable investigation priority.
8. Communicate the expected review timeline.
9. Escalate cases meeting fraud, legal, enterprise, or high-value thresholds.
10. Document the final decision and all financial adjustments.

### 2.3 Investigation Procedure

The reviewing team should perform the following sequence:

1. Match the disputed amount to the Company's billing record.
2. Validate the invoice or transaction date.
3. Determine the service, subscription, product, or usage associated with the charge.
4. Review applicable pricing, discounts, taxes, credits, and contractual terms.
5. Review cancellation, downgrade, renewal, or refund activity.
6. Compare internal transaction records with payment processor records.
7. Determine whether the charge is valid, partially valid, or invalid.
8. Calculate any approved adjustment.
9. Record the decision rationale.
10. Issue the applicable credit, refund, correction, or explanatory response.

### 2.4 Resolution Outcomes

A dispute may result in:

- **No adjustment:** the charge is supported by Company records and applicable terms.
- **Full refund:** the disputed amount is refunded.
- **Partial refund:** only the unsupported or incorrectly billed portion is refunded.
- **Account credit:** a credit is applied instead of a payment-method refund where permitted.
- **Invoice correction:** billing records are corrected.
- **Future billing adjustment:** an identified recurring error is corrected for subsequent invoices.
- **Further investigation:** additional evidence or specialist review is required.

### 2.5 Payment-Method Disputes and Chargebacks

Customers should contact Company before initiating a payment-provider chargeback when practical, because Company may be able to investigate and resolve a billing error faster.

A payment-provider chargeback may trigger:

- Temporary suspension of the normal billing-dispute process for the same transaction
- Submission of transaction evidence to the payment provider
- Additional fraud or account-security review
- Restriction on duplicate refunds for the same transaction

Company does not issue a second refund for a transaction already reversed through a card-network or payment-provider dispute.

## 3. Exceptions, Restrictions & Special Cases

### 3.1 Late Disputes

Disputes submitted after the standard window may be rejected unless:

- Required by applicable law;
- The customer demonstrates that the charge could not reasonably have been identified earlier;
- Company determines that an administrative or billing error warrants review;
- A contractual enterprise agreement provides a different deadline.

Late submission does not create an automatic refund entitlement.

### 3.2 Unauthorized or Fraudulent Charges

Unrecognized charges are investigated separately from ordinary dissatisfaction with a valid purchase.

Company may require additional verification, including:

- Identity confirmation
- Account-security validation
- Confirmation of known devices or sessions
- Payment-method verification using only permitted partial identifiers
- Confirmation that the customer did not authorize another account user

For suspected account compromise, Company may temporarily restrict account access or require credential resets.

### 3.3 Duplicate Charges

When two charges appear similar, Company first verifies whether they represent:

- A true duplicate transaction;
- A separate subscription;
- A recurring renewal;
- An authorization hold and later settled transaction;
- Different invoices billed on the same date;
- Different currencies or tax treatments.

An authorization hold that was never settled is not treated as a duplicate settled charge.

### 3.4 Canceled Subscription Disputes

A charge may remain valid after cancellation when:

- Cancellation occurred after the renewal or billing timestamp;
- The applicable subscription terms require payment through the current billing period;
- The customer canceled renewal but retained access through the paid period;
- A prior invoice remained unpaid and was subsequently collected.

A charge generated **before** an effective cancellation timestamp is not automatically invalid merely because the cancellation request was submitted later that day.

### 3.5 Promotional Discounts

A promotional dispute may be denied when eligibility conditions were not met, including:

- The promotion was limited to new customers;
- A required promotional code was not entered;
- The promotional period expired;
- Multiple promotions were combined contrary to the offer terms;
- The account or transaction was excluded from the promotion.

Promotional credits are generally not converted to cash unless the applicable offer expressly permits it.

### 3.6 Non-Refundable Charges

Unless applicable law or a specific contractual provision requires otherwise, the following may be **non-refundable**:

- Clearly disclosed non-refundable fees
- Certain completed usage charges
- Third-party pass-through charges
- Taxes already remitted to a government authority, where reversal is not permitted
- Fees associated with optional expedited or completed services when expressly identified as non-refundable

A billing error involving a non-refundable line item may still qualify for correction if Company incorrectly charged the item.

### 3.7 Enterprise Accounts

Enterprise customers may be subject to:

- Contract-specific dispute periods
- Purchase-order requirements
- Account-manager review
- Consolidated invoicing procedures
- Different service-level commitments
- Contractually defined credit and refund mechanisms

Where an enterprise agreement conflicts with this policy, the **applicable signed agreement controls** to the extent legally enforceable.

### 3.8 International and Multi-Currency Transactions

For disputes involving currencies other than the Company's billing currency:

- The original transaction currency is used when available.
- Refunds are normally returned to the original payment method and processed in the transaction currency where supported.
- Exchange-rate movement between purchase and refund may cause the customer's bank statement to show a different converted amount.
- Company is not responsible for foreign-exchange movements imposed by the customer's card issuer or bank unless required by applicable law.
- Cross-border payment processing may add additional banking or processing time.

### 3.9 Tax Disputes

Tax disputes may require proof of applicable tax-exempt or tax-registered status.

A tax correction may depend on:

- Billing address;
- Place of supply;
- Tax jurisdiction;
- Valid exemption documentation;
- Date on which qualifying documentation was received.

Tax amounts cannot be removed solely because a customer believes the charge "should not have tax" without sufficient jurisdictional evidence.

### 3.10 Abuse and Repetitive Disputes

Company may escalate or restrict dispute privileges where it detects:

- Repeated unsupported claims;
- Deliberate manipulation of billing records;
- Multiple claims for the same transaction;
- Refund exploitation;
- Fraud indicators;
- Systematic misuse of promotions or credits.

Such restrictions do not override mandatory consumer protections or applicable law.

## 4. Processing Times, Fees & Financial Terms

### 4.1 Standard Processing Timeline

| Activity | Target Timeline |
|---|---:|
| Dispute acknowledgment | Within **1 business day** |
| Standard initial investigation | **3–5 business days** |
| Complex investigation | Up to **10 business days** |
| Fraud/security review | Typically **5–10 business days** |
| Secondary review | **3–7 business days** |
| Internal refund initiation after approval | Within **2 business days** |
| Card/bank posting after refund initiation | Typically **5–10 business days** |
| International refund posting | Typically **5–15 business days** |

Processing times begin after Company receives the minimum information required to investigate.

### 4.2 Temporary Credits

Company may issue a temporary account credit during an investigation when:

- The dispute appears credible;
- The billing error is operationally verifiable;
- The amount qualifies under internal authorization limits.

A temporary credit is **not a final determination** and may be reversed if the charge is ultimately found valid.

### 4.3 Fees

Company does not charge a customer for submitting a valid billing dispute.

No "dispute filing fee" may be represented as a condition for Company review.

Where a customer independently initiates a bank, card, or payment-provider dispute, the provider's own fees, foreign-exchange charges, or banking costs may apply independently of Company policy.

### 4.4 Refund Calculation

Unless otherwise required:

**Refund Amount = Unsupported/Invalid Charge Amount − Any Non-Reversible Amounts Permitted Under the Applicable Terms**

Partial refunds may be issued when only part of an invoice is disputed successfully.

### 4.5 Currency Handling

Refunds normally return to the original payment method.

Where the original method is unavailable, Company may offer another permitted refund mechanism after verification. Additional identity or fraud checks may be required before changing the destination of a refund.

### 4.6 No Duplicate Recovery

Customers may not recover the same disputed amount twice through combinations of:

- Company refund;
- Account credit;
- Payment-provider chargeback;
- Bank reversal;
- Duplicate transaction correction.

Where duplicate recovery occurs, Company may reverse the excess credit or pursue reconciliation consistent with applicable law.

## 5. Escalations & Customer Responsibilities

### 5.1 Customer Responsibilities

Customers must:

- Submit disputes within the applicable deadline.
- Provide accurate and complete information.
- Cooperate with reasonable identity or account verification.
- Submit requested evidence within **7 calendar days**, unless another deadline is provided.
- Avoid submitting duplicate claims for the same transaction.
- Promptly notify Company if the issue is resolved through another channel.
- Protect passwords, payment credentials, authentication codes, and account-security information.

### 5.2 Missing Information

If required information is missing, Company may place the case into a pending state.

If the customer does not respond within **7 calendar days** after a documented request for information, Company may close the case administratively. Administrative closure does not necessarily prevent resubmission when the applicable dispute window remains open.

### 5.3 Escalation Levels

| Level | Trigger | Target Review |
|---|---|---:|
| Level 1 — Billing Support | Standard billing dispute | **3–5 business days** |
| Level 2 — Billing Specialist | Complex, repeated, or disputed outcome | **3–7 business days** |
| Level 3 — Risk/Fraud | Unauthorized payment or fraud indicator | **5–10 business days** |
| Level 4 — Legal/Executive | Legal demand, regulatory issue, material contractual dispute | Case-specific |

### 5.4 Secondary Review

A customer may request a secondary review when:

- Material evidence was not considered;
- The customer can provide new documentation;
- The disputed amount was calculated incorrectly;
- The customer identifies a relevant contract or policy provision that was overlooked.

A request for secondary review should include the original case number and the specific reason the decision should be reconsidered.

### 5.5 Enterprise Escalation

Enterprise customers should use the contractual escalation path, which may include:

1. Billing support;
2. Account manager;
3. Finance or accounts-receivable specialist;
4. Contractual escalation contact;
5. Legal escalation where required.

Contractual dispute deadlines and notice requirements must be observed.

### 5.6 Record Retention

Company may retain billing-dispute records, including communications, transaction identifiers, invoices, and decision records, for the period required by applicable law, contractual obligations, accounting requirements, fraud prevention needs, or legitimate operational purposes.

### 5.7 Policy Priority

Where this policy conflicts with mandatory law, applicable regulatory requirements, or a signed customer agreement, the mandatory legal or contractual requirement controls to the extent of the conflict.

> **Critical Warning:** Customers should never send full card numbers, CVV/security codes, passwords, one-time authentication codes, or private keys to Company support personnel. Company support will not require these credentials to investigate an ordinary billing dispute.
