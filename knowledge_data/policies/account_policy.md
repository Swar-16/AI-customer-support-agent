## 1. General Overview & Eligibility Criteria

### 1.1 Purpose

This policy governs the creation, maintenance, access, security, recovery, modification, suspension, and closure of customer accounts operated by the Company.

The account system is intended to provide a persistent identity for customers, preserve account-related records, enable authenticated access to Company services, and support security, billing, order, subscription, and customer-support workflows.

### 1.2 Account Eligibility

A customer may create an account when all applicable requirements below are satisfied:

| Requirement | Standard Rule |
|---|---|
| Minimum age | **18 years** unless a separately supported service explicitly permits a younger user with legally valid authorization |
| Valid email | Required for standard account creation |
| Phone number | Required when requested for account security, high-risk actions, or regional verification |
| Password | Must satisfy the Company's current password-security requirements |
| Unique identity | One person must not create accounts for impersonation, fraud, abuse, or circumvention of restrictions |
| Payment information | Required only for products or services that require payment |
| Geographic availability | Account services may be restricted in jurisdictions where the Company does not operate or cannot legally provide the relevant service |
| Verification | The Company may require identity, email, phone, business, or payment verification before activating certain features |

### 1.3 Account Ownership

The person who creates an account is responsible for maintaining accurate account information and protecting authentication credentials.

An account is not transferable unless the Company expressly authorizes a transfer in writing or through a supported account-transfer workflow.

Login credentials, verification codes, recovery links, API credentials, and security tokens must not be shared with another person.

### 1.4 Required Account Information

Customers must maintain accurate information reasonably necessary to administer the account, including:

- Legal or account display name, where requested.
- Email address.
- Phone number, when enabled or required.
- Billing and shipping information where applicable.
- Business information for business accounts.
- Payment information where payment is required.

Changes to material account information may trigger additional verification.

### 1.5 Email Verification

Where email verification is required:

1. The Company sends a verification message to the submitted email address.
2. The verification link generally remains valid for **24 hours**.
3. The customer may request a new verification message after expiration.
4. Repeated requests may be rate-limited for security.
5. An account may remain restricted until verification is completed.

### 1.6 Account Security

The Company may apply automated security controls including:

- Login attempt rate limiting.
- Multi-factor authentication requirements.
- Device or session verification.
- Temporary security holds.
- CAPTCHA or equivalent anti-abuse controls.
- Identity verification.
- Payment verification.
- Password reset requirements.

> **Critical Warning:** The Company will never require a customer to disclose a one-time password, security code, recovery code, or complete password through ordinary customer-support channels.

## 2. Step-by-Step Operating Procedures

### 2.1 Creating a Standard Account

1. Customer enters the required account information.
2. Customer accepts the applicable Terms and Privacy Notice.
3. Customer creates authentication credentials.
4. Company performs automated validation and anti-abuse checks.
5. Company sends an email verification message when required.
6. Customer verifies the email address.
7. Company activates the account or applicable account features.
8. Additional verification may be requested before high-risk functionality becomes available.

### 2.2 Updating Account Information

1. Sign in to the account using an authenticated session.
2. Open **Account Settings** or the applicable profile-management interface.
3. Select the information to be changed.
4. Enter the new information.
5. Complete any requested verification.
6. Save the change.
7. Confirm that the updated information appears correctly.

For security-sensitive changes, the Company may require re-authentication, multi-factor authentication, or confirmation through the existing verified contact method.

### 2.3 Changing an Email Address

1. Customer signs in to the account.
2. Customer opens the account's email settings.
3. Customer submits the new email address.
4. Company may request the current password or other authentication.
5. Company sends a verification message to the new email address.
6. Customer completes verification within **24 hours**, where the link is time-limited.
7. The new address becomes the account's verified email.
8. Security notifications may be sent to the previous email address where technically and legally appropriate.

A new email address does not create a new contractual relationship by itself; the existing account remains associated with its account history unless the Company states otherwise.

### 2.4 Password Reset

1. Customer selects **Forgot Password** or the equivalent recovery option.
2. Customer provides the account email or other supported identifier.
3. Company performs automated anti-abuse checks.
4. A reset message is sent if the account is eligible for password recovery.
5. Customer opens the reset link.
6. Customer creates a new password meeting current security requirements.
7. Existing sessions may be invalidated as a security precaution.
8. Customer signs in using the new password.

Password-reset links are generally valid for **30 minutes** unless the interface states a different expiration period.

### 2.5 Lost Access to the Registered Email

When a customer can no longer access the email address associated with the account:

1. Customer attempts standard password recovery.
2. If recovery cannot be completed, customer contacts Customer Support.
3. Customer provides the requested account identifiers and ownership evidence.
4. Support reviews the request using account-security procedures.
5. Additional identity or transaction verification may be requested.
6. If ownership is satisfactorily established, the Company may restore access or update the verified contact information.
7. If ownership cannot be established, the Company may decline the request.

> **Security Rule:** Customer Support must not bypass ownership verification solely because the requester knows the account name, previous email address, order number, or payment amount.

### 2.6 Enabling Multi-Factor Authentication

1. Customer opens **Security Settings**.
2. Customer selects an available MFA method.
3. Customer completes enrollment.
4. Company confirms successful registration.
5. Customer should store recovery codes securely where provided.
6. Customer must not share MFA codes with any third party.

The Company may require MFA for privileged, high-value, or high-risk actions.

### 2.7 Closing an Account

1. Customer signs in.
2. Customer reviews active subscriptions, open orders, credits, refunds, claims, or other pending transactions.
3. Customer initiates account closure through supported settings or Customer Support.
4. Company may require authentication or additional verification.
5. Any legally required records or transaction records are retained according to applicable retention requirements.
6. Active services may be cancelled or remain active according to their separate contractual terms.
7. Account access is disabled after closure processing.
8. Customer receives confirmation where a confirmation channel is available.

Account deletion does not automatically reverse completed transactions or erase records that the Company is legally required to retain.

### 2.8 Account Recovery After Suspected Compromise

1. Customer immediately changes the password if access remains available.
2. Customer signs out unknown sessions or devices where the feature is available.
3. Customer enables or re-enables MFA.
4. Customer contacts Support if unauthorized activity is suspected.
5. Support may place a temporary security restriction on the account.
6. The Company investigates relevant security events.
7. Additional verification may be required before restoring unrestricted access.
8. Where unauthorized transactions are identified, the customer must follow the applicable payment, refund, or dispute process.

## 3. Exceptions, Restrictions & Special Cases

### 3.1 Duplicate Accounts

Customers should not create duplicate accounts to bypass:

- Account restrictions.
- Promotional limitations.
- Subscription limits.
- Payment controls.
- Identity verification.
- Fraud-prevention measures.
- Geographic restrictions.

The Company may consolidate, restrict, or suspend duplicate accounts where permitted by law and applicable terms.

### 3.2 Fraud, Abuse, and Security Restrictions

The Company may temporarily restrict or suspend an account when automated or manual controls identify indicators of:

- Account takeover.
- Identity fraud.
- Payment fraud.
- Credential abuse.
- Automated abuse.
- Repeated policy circumvention.
- Unauthorized access.
- Suspicious account creation patterns.

Security restrictions may be imposed before customer notification when advance notice would materially increase security risk.

### 3.3 International Accounts

Account availability and features may differ by country or region.

Restrictions may apply because of:

- Local law.
- Sanctions or export controls.
- Tax requirements.
- Payment-network limitations.
- Identity-verification requirements.
- Data-residency requirements.
- Product availability.

A customer traveling internationally may be required to complete additional verification when access originates from an unusual location.

### 3.4 Business and Enterprise Accounts

Enterprise or business accounts may be governed by negotiated agreements that supersede conflicting standard operational terms.

Enterprise administrators may have additional permissions, including user provisioning, access management, domain verification, and account recovery controls.

An enterprise administrator cannot necessarily access individual customer passwords, MFA secrets, or private authentication credentials.

### 3.5 Deceased or Incapacitated Account Holder

The Company may require legal documentation before processing account-access or account-transfer requests involving a deceased or legally incapacitated account holder.

Documents may include:

- Government-issued identification.
- Death certificate or equivalent official record.
- Court order.
- Executor, administrator, guardian, or equivalent legal authority.
- Other documentation reasonably required under applicable law.

### 3.6 Name Changes

A customer may update a display or legal name through supported account settings. The Company may require documentation where the change affects billing, tax, regulated services, business ownership, or identity verification.

### 3.7 Promotional Abuse

Promotions limited to one account, customer, household, device, payment method, or other eligibility unit may not be duplicated by creating additional accounts.

The Company may cancel promotional benefits obtained through circumvention.

### 3.8 Account Closure During Open Transactions

An account cannot necessarily be immediately deleted when it has:

- An unresolved chargeback.
- A pending refund.
- An open fraud investigation.
- An active legal dispute.
- An unfulfilled order.
- An outstanding balance.
- A compliance or regulatory hold.

The Company may complete required transaction processing before final closure.

### 3.9 Non-Accessible Accounts

An account that has been compromised, restricted, or locked may not be restored solely upon request. Security verification takes precedence over convenience.

### 3.10 Prohibited Credentials

The following must never be submitted to Customer Support unless an explicitly authenticated security workflow requires otherwise:

- Plain-text passwords.
- One-time authentication codes.
- MFA recovery codes.
- Private API keys.
- Payment card PINs.
- Authentication secrets belonging to another person.

## 4. Processing Times, Fees & Financial Terms

### 4.1 Standard Account Operations

| Operation | Standard Target | Possible Exception |
|---|---:|---|
| Email verification message | Usually within **5 minutes** | Email-provider delay or anti-abuse rate limiting |
| Password reset message | Usually within **5 minutes** | Security review or delivery delay |
| Password reset link validity | **30 minutes** | Interface may specify a different period |
| Email-change verification link | **24 hours** | Security policy may require reissue |
| Standard profile update | Usually immediate | Additional verification may delay completion |
| MFA enrollment | Usually immediate | Device verification may be required |
| Basic account closure | Usually within **24 hours** | Open transactions or legal holds may extend processing |
| Manual account recovery | Usually **1–3 business days** | Complex ownership or fraud reviews may take longer |
| Security investigation | Target **3–7 business days** | Complex incidents may require additional time |

These are operational targets rather than guaranteed completion times unless an applicable service agreement provides otherwise.

### 4.2 Account Fees

The Company does **not charge a standard fee solely to create, maintain, or close a standard customer account**, unless a specific paid product, service, or contractual plan expressly states otherwise.

No "account recovery fee" may be charged merely because a customer forgot a password.

Separate service-level fees, subscription charges, transaction fees, or enterprise charges remain governed by their applicable commercial terms.

### 4.3 Currency Handling

Account records may display transactions in the currency associated with the original transaction.

Where a financial amount is converted:

- The applicable exchange rate may be determined by the payment processor, financial institution, or Company's transaction system.
- Foreign-exchange costs, if any, may be charged by the relevant payment provider.
- The displayed account currency does not necessarily change the currency of the underlying transaction.
- Refunds are ordinarily processed using the transaction's applicable refund method and currency, subject to payment-network constraints.

### 4.4 Refunds and Account Credits

Closing an account does not automatically:

- Cancel pending refunds.
- Convert non-refundable credits into cash.
- Reverse completed transactions.
- Waive outstanding balances.

Any refund entitlement remains subject to the applicable Refund Policy, order terms, subscription terms, payment-network rules, and applicable law.

### 4.5 Dormant Accounts

Where permitted by applicable law and contractual terms, the Company may send notices to accounts that remain unused for an extended period.

The Company may require re-authentication or verification before restoring access to a dormant or security-restricted account.

## 5. Escalations & Customer Responsibilities

### 5.1 Customer Responsibilities

Customers are responsible for:

1. Providing accurate account information.
2. Maintaining access to the verified email address where possible.
3. Using a unique and appropriately secure password.
4. Protecting MFA credentials and recovery codes.
5. Promptly reporting suspected unauthorized access.
6. Reviewing security notifications and account activity.
7. Keeping business-account administrator information current.
8. Responding to reasonable verification requests.
9. Not using duplicate accounts to circumvent restrictions.
10. Maintaining records relevant to disputes, including order numbers and transaction dates.

### 5.2 Documentation for Manual Review

Depending on the issue, Support may request:

| Issue | Potential Documentation |
|---|---|
| Account ownership | Account email, prior account details, transaction information |
| Lost email access | Ownership evidence and replacement contact information |
| Enterprise administration | Authorization from an approved organization administrator |
| Identity mismatch | Government-issued identification or other approved documentation |
| Suspected fraud | Transaction details, dates, device information, and relevant evidence |
| Legal account transfer | Court order or other legally sufficient authority |

Customers should redact unnecessary sensitive information before submitting documentation.

### 5.3 Escalation Path

Standard escalation sequence:

1. **Customer Support** performs initial troubleshooting.
2. **Account Operations** handles account-state or administrative exceptions.
3. **Trust & Safety / Security Operations** handles suspected compromise, abuse, and fraud.
4. **Payments or Billing Operations** handles account-linked financial disputes.
5. **Legal or Compliance** handles legally sensitive requests, regulatory matters, or formal legal demands.

### 5.4 Disputes

A customer should first provide:

- Account email or recognized account identifier.
- Description of the issue.
- Relevant dates and times.
- Transaction or order identifiers where applicable.
- Screenshots or other evidence when useful.
- The requested resolution.

Customers should not submit passwords, one-time codes, or unrelated sensitive data.

### 5.5 Service-Level Expectations

Where no separate contractual SLA applies:

- Initial standard support acknowledgment target: **within 1 business day**.
- Manual account-recovery review target: **1–3 business days**.
- Complex security investigation target: **3–7 business days**.
- Legal/compliance review: timeframe depends on the request and applicable legal requirements.

### 5.6 Final Authority

Where this Account Policy conflicts with:

- Applicable law,
- A separately executed enterprise agreement,
- A product-specific contractual term,
- A legally binding regulatory requirement,

the higher-priority legal or contractual requirement controls to the extent of the conflict.

The Company may amend this policy prospectively. Material changes should be communicated through the Company's standard customer-notification channels where required.
