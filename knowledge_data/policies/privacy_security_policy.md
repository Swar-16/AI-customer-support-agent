## 1. General Overview & Eligibility Criteria

### 1.1 Purpose

The Company collects, uses, stores, discloses, and protects personal information only for legitimate business, contractual, security, legal, and service-delivery purposes.

The Company applies the principles of:

- **Data minimization**
- **Purpose limitation**
- **Accuracy**
- **Storage limitation**
- **Confidentiality and integrity**
- **Accountability**
- **Privacy by design and by default**

The Company does not sell personal information for monetary consideration where prohibited by applicable law.

### 1.2 Information Covered

Depending on the service used, information may include:

| Data Category | Examples | Typical Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity Information** | Name, username, date of birth where required | Account creation and verification |
| **Contact Information** | Email, phone number, postal address | Communications and service delivery |
| **Transaction Information** | Orders, invoices, refunds, payment status | Contract fulfillment and accounting |
| **Device Information** | IP address, browser, OS, device identifiers | Security, fraud prevention, diagnostics |
| **Usage Information** | Login events, feature usage, session data | Product operation, analytics, security |
| **Support Information** | Tickets, messages, attachments, call records where permitted | Customer support and dispute handling |
| **Verification Information** | Information required for identity or fraud checks | Security, AML/KYC obligations where applicable |
| **Preference Information** | Communication and privacy preferences | Preference management |
| **Security Information** | Authentication logs, security events, risk signals | Account and platform protection |

Sensitive or special-category information is processed only where there is a lawful basis and an operational requirement to do so.

### 1.3 Eligibility

Privacy requests may generally be submitted by:

1. The individual to whom the personal information relates;
2. An authorized representative acting on that individual's behalf; or
3. A business administrator or authorized enterprise contact where the information is controlled by an organization.

The Company may require reasonable verification before fulfilling a request.

**Identity verification is separate from authentication.** Successful login alone does not guarantee that a high-risk privacy request will be approved.

### 1.4 Legal Bases and Processing Purposes

Depending on jurisdiction, processing may rely on one or more of:

- **Contractual necessity**
- **Legal obligation**
- **Legitimate interests**
- **Consent**
- **Protection of vital interests**
- Other legally recognized grounds

Common purposes include:

- Providing and improving requested services;
- Processing transactions;
- Detecting fraud, abuse, and unauthorized access;
- Maintaining service reliability and availability;
- Responding to support requests;
- Meeting tax, accounting, sanctions, legal, and regulatory requirements;
- Sending service-related communications;
- Sending marketing communications where permitted and appropriately authorized;
- Measuring product performance and security.

### 1.5 Consent and Withdrawal

Where processing depends on consent, the customer may withdraw consent using the applicable privacy or communication settings.

Withdrawal:

- Does **not** retroactively invalidate processing that occurred before withdrawal;
- May not stop processing required by law or necessary to perform a contract;
- May affect availability of optional features;
- Will generally be reflected in Company systems within **72 hours**.

Marketing opt-out requests are normally processed within **24 hours**, although individual communications already queued for delivery may arrive before suppression completes.

### 1.6 Data Accuracy

Customers are responsible for keeping account and contact information reasonably accurate.

Customers may correct profile information through account settings or by submitting a verified privacy request.

Where a correction affects a legal, financial, tax, fraud, or regulatory record, the Company may retain the original record while recording the corrected value.

### 1.7 Data Retention Principles

The Company retains personal information only for as long as reasonably necessary for the purpose collected, contractual requirements, security needs, dispute resolution, and applicable legal obligations.

Default operational targets include:

| Data Type | Default Retention Target | Key Exception |
|---|---:|---|
| Active account profile | While account remains active | Legal retention may extend period |
| Inactive account data | Up to **24 months** after last meaningful activity | Longer period may apply for legal or fraud obligations |
| Support tickets | Up to **24 months** after closure | Litigation, fraud, or regulatory hold may extend retention |
| Transaction and billing records | Up to **7 years** | Longer retention required by local law may apply |
| Security/access logs | Generally **12 months** | Critical security investigations may require longer retention |
| Fraud-risk records | Generally **5 years** from last relevant event | Legal/regulatory requirement may extend period |
| Consent and preference records | Duration necessary to demonstrate compliance | May be retained after account closure |
| Backups | Typically **35–90 days** depending on system | Backup deletion follows technical lifecycle |

Deletion from active systems does **not** necessarily mean immediate destruction from all backup media.

## 2. Step-by-Step Operating Procedures

### 2.1 Submit a Privacy Request

Customers may submit requests using the Company's privacy request channel or authenticated account settings.

#### Step 1 — Select Request Type

Available request types may include:

- Access
- Correction
- Deletion
- Restriction
- Objection
- Data portability
- Consent withdrawal
- Marketing opt-out
- Security or privacy incident report

#### Step 2 — Provide Required Information

The requester should provide:

- Full name associated with the account;
- Account email or customer identifier;
- Description of the request;
- Relevant date range, if known;
- Specific data or processing activity concerned;
- Authorized-representative documentation where applicable.

#### Step 3 — Identity Verification

The Company may request one or more verification methods:

- Authenticated account confirmation;
- One-time verification code;
- Confirmation of account-specific information;
- Additional documentation where legally necessary.

For sensitive requests, identity documents may be requested only through an approved secure channel.

**Never send passwords, authentication codes, private keys, or card security codes through ordinary email or chat.**

#### Step 4 — Request Validation

The Privacy & Security Office classifies the request and determines:

1. Whether the requester is eligible;
2. Whether verification is sufficient;
3. Whether an applicable legal exception exists;
4. Whether another business unit or processor must be engaged.

#### Step 5 — Fulfillment

The Company generally responds to validated privacy requests within:

- **30 calendar days** where the applicable law requires a one-month period;
- Or the shorter period required by applicable local law.

Where law permits an extension because of complexity or request volume, the Company will provide notice explaining the extension before the initial deadline expires.

#### Step 6 — Completion Notice

The customer receives confirmation of:

- Action taken;
- Data categories involved;
- Applicable limitations or exceptions;
- Any remaining retention requirement;
- Appeal or escalation options where available.

---

### 2.2 Request a Copy of Personal Data

A verified access request generally follows this process:

1. Submit an access request.
2. Complete identity verification.
3. The Company identifies relevant data stores.
4. Information is filtered to remove information the Company is not legally permitted to disclose.
5. The Company prepares an export or response.
6. The export is delivered through a secure authenticated portal or another approved secure method.

Standard exports may be provided in **CSV, JSON, or another machine-readable format** when technically feasible.

The Company does not provide another person's personal information merely because that information appears in the requester's account or communications.

---

### 2.3 Request Account or Data Deletion

To request deletion:

1. Submit a verified deletion request.
2. Confirm the account or information to be deleted.
3. The Company checks legal, financial, security, fraud, and contractual retention requirements.
4. Data eligible for deletion is removed or irreversibly anonymized.
5. Downstream deletion instructions are issued to applicable processors where required.
6. The Company sends completion confirmation.

Target completion is **within 30 calendar days after successful verification**, subject to lawful exceptions.

Deletion may be refused or limited where retention is required for:

- Tax or accounting obligations;
- Legal claims;
- Fraud prevention;
- Security investigations;
- Regulatory obligations;
- Contractual recordkeeping;
- Exercising or defending legal rights.

---

### 2.4 Report Suspected Account Compromise

Customers should report suspected compromise immediately.

#### Customer procedure

1. Change the password from a trusted device.
2. Sign out other active sessions where the feature is available.
3. Revoke unknown API keys, application sessions, or connected integrations.
4. Enable multi-factor authentication.
5. Review recent account activity.
6. Contact Company support/security through the approved channel.
7. Provide the approximate time, device, location, suspicious activity, and any relevant screenshots.

For urgent security issues, customers should use the Company's designated security escalation mechanism rather than ordinary marketing or billing channels.

### 2.5 Security Incident Handling

When the Company receives a credible security incident report:

1. The report is assigned a security incident identifier.
2. Triage begins as soon as practicable, targeting **within 4 hours** for high-severity reports received through approved security channels.
3. Security personnel assess scope, affected systems, and potential data exposure.
4. Access credentials or sessions may be suspended as a containment measure.
5. Relevant evidence is preserved.
6. Affected vendors are engaged where necessary.
7. Legal, privacy, and regulatory assessments are performed.
8. Customer or regulator notifications are issued when legally required or operationally appropriate.
9. Remediation and recovery actions are documented.
10. A post-incident review is completed for material incidents.

Not every reported security event constitutes a confirmed personal-data breach.

---

### 2.6 Security Controls

The Company maintains administrative, physical, and technical safeguards proportionate to risk.

Controls may include:

- Encryption in transit using modern TLS configurations;
- Encryption at rest for systems containing sensitive customer data where technically supported;
- Role-based access control;
- Least-privilege permissions;
- Multi-factor authentication for privileged access;
- Centralized audit logging;
- Endpoint security controls;
- Vulnerability scanning;
- Security monitoring and alerting;
- Secure software-development practices;
- Backup and disaster-recovery controls;
- Vendor security assessments;
- Employee security and privacy training.

Privileged access is subject to additional controls and logging.

### 2.7 Third-Party Service Providers

The Company may use service providers for functions such as:

- Cloud hosting;
- Payment processing;
- Customer support;
- Analytics;
- Communications;
- Fraud prevention;
- Identity verification;
- Security monitoring.

Where applicable, contractual controls address confidentiality, permitted processing, security, incident notification, and data handling obligations.

Third parties are not authorized to use Company customer data for unrelated purposes except where independently permitted by law and contract.

## 3. Exceptions, Restrictions & Special Cases

### 3.1 Legal Retention Exception

A deletion request does not automatically override mandatory recordkeeping.

For example, transaction records may need to be retained for **up to 7 years** where necessary for accounting, tax, audit, or legal obligations.

### 3.2 Fraud and Security Exception

The Company may retain limited information after account deletion where reasonably necessary to:

- Detect repeat fraud;
- Prevent abuse;
- Investigate security incidents;
- Enforce platform rules;
- Protect customers or the Company;
- Comply with legal obligations.

Such information should be restricted to the minimum reasonably required.

### 3.3 Litigation or Legal Hold

If information is subject to a legal hold, deletion may be suspended until the hold is released.

A legal hold may apply even when the normal retention period has expired.

### 3.4 Enterprise Customer Exception

For enterprise accounts, the organization may be the controller or business responsible for certain employee/user information.

In those cases:

- The enterprise agreement may determine data roles;
- The enterprise administrator may control certain account data;
- Individual requests may need to be routed through the enterprise;
- The Company's direct deletion authority may be restricted by the enterprise contract.

### 3.5 International Data Transfers

Customer information may be processed in countries other than the customer's country.

International transfers may rely on:

- Adequacy decisions;
- Standard contractual clauses;
- Other approved contractual safeguards;
- Binding corporate rules where applicable;
- Consent or another lawful transfer mechanism where legally permitted.

A transfer mechanism may differ by country or customer type.

### 3.6 Government and Law-Enforcement Requests

The Company may disclose information when legally required.

Where legally permitted, the Company may:

- Verify the requesting authority;
- Narrow the disclosure to responsive data;
- Seek clarification of overbroad requests;
- Challenge unlawful or disproportionate requests.

The Company will not necessarily notify an affected customer when notification is prohibited or would materially interfere with a lawful investigation.

### 3.7 Emergency Disclosure

Information may be disclosed without normal customer authorization where necessary to address an imminent threat of death, serious bodily harm, or another legally recognized emergency.

### 3.8 Children's Data

The Company's services are not intended for users below the minimum age permitted by applicable law unless expressly supported.

Where the Company becomes aware that personal data was collected unlawfully from a child, it may suspend the account and take appropriate steps to delete or otherwise process the information as required by law.

### 3.9 Anonymized and Aggregated Information

Information that has been irreversibly anonymized so that individuals can no longer reasonably be identified may be retained for:

- Statistical analysis;
- Product improvement;
- Security analysis;
- Business reporting.

Anonymized information is not treated as personal information where applicable law recognizes the anonymization as effective.

### 3.10 Cookies and Similar Technologies

The Company may use cookies, SDKs, pixels, local storage, and similar technologies.

These may be used for:

- Essential functionality;
- Authentication;
- Security;
- Preferences;
- Analytics;
- Advertising or measurement where permitted.

Where consent is required, non-essential technologies should not be activated until the required consent is obtained.

### 3.11 Do-Not-Track and Global Privacy Controls

Where legally required or technically supported, the Company may recognize browser- or device-based privacy signals as requests to limit certain data uses.

The treatment of privacy signals may vary by jurisdiction.

### 3.12 Payment Card Data

The Company generally does not store full payment card security codes.

Payment information may be processed by approved payment providers, and the Company may retain limited transaction identifiers, payment status, masked account information, and related billing records.

Customers must never provide a full card number or security code in a support ticket unless an approved secure payment form expressly requests it.

### 3.13 Authentication Credentials

Passwords should be stored using industry-standard cryptographic password-hashing mechanisms rather than reversible plaintext storage.

The Company support team will **never require disclosure of a password or multi-factor authentication code**.

### 3.14 Data Subject Request Abuse

The Company may take reasonable measures against:

- Repeatedly duplicative requests;
- Manifestly unfounded requests;
- Fraudulent requests;
- Requests made to disrupt operations.

Where permitted by law, a reasonable administrative fee may be charged for excessive or manifestly unfounded requests. The Company will not use fees to improperly discourage valid privacy rights.

## 4. Processing Times, Fees & Financial Terms

### 4.1 Standard Privacy Request Timelines

| Request / Event | Target Processing Time |
|---|---:|
| Marketing opt-out | **Within 24 hours** |
| Consent/preference update | **Within 72 hours** |
| Account correction | **1–5 business days** where manual review is required |
| Security report acknowledgment | Target **within 4 hours** for high-severity reports |
| Standard privacy request | **Within 30 calendar days** after verification |
| Complex privacy request extension | As permitted by applicable law |
| Deletion from active systems | Usually **within 30 calendar days** after verification |
| Backup expiration | Typically **35–90 days**, depending on system |
| Security incident investigation | Severity-dependent; no universal fixed completion period |

### 4.2 Privacy Request Fees

Routine privacy requests are generally **free of charge**.

A fee or reasonable-cost recovery may be considered only where:

1. Applicable law permits it;
2. The request is manifestly unfounded, excessive, or repetitive; and
3. The Company provides an explanation before imposing the charge.

No fee is charged merely because a customer exercises a valid statutory privacy right.

### 4.3 Currency

Where a fee is legally permissible, the applicable currency is determined by the customer's contractual billing region unless otherwise required by law.

Examples:

- United States billing region: **USD**
- United Kingdom billing region: **GBP**
- Eurozone billing region: **EUR**
- India billing region: **INR**

Taxes or government charges may apply where legally required.

### 4.4 Data Portability

Where a portability right applies, the Company aims to provide commonly used structured data formats such as **CSV or JSON**.

Portability may be limited where providing the information would adversely affect another person's rights or disclose protected confidential information.

## 5. Escalations & Customer Responsibilities

### 5.1 Customer Responsibilities

Customers must:

- Keep credentials confidential;
- Use unique passwords;
- Enable multi-factor authentication where available;
- Maintain current contact information;
- Report suspected compromise promptly;
- Use official Company support and privacy channels;
- Avoid sending passwords, MFA codes, private keys, or full payment credentials;
- Provide truthful and sufficiently detailed information in privacy requests;
- Complete identity verification when reasonably required;
- Protect exported personal-data files after download.

### 5.2 Security Escalation Levels

| Severity | Example | Target Initial Handling |
|---|---|---:|
| **Critical (P1)** | Confirmed unauthorized access involving sensitive data | **Within 4 hours** |
| **High (P2)** | Suspected account takeover or significant security weakness | **Within 1 business day** |
| **Medium (P3)** | Suspicious activity with limited apparent impact | **Within 2 business days** |
| **Low (P4)** | General security/privacy question | **Within 5 business days** |

These are operational targets and do not override legal notification deadlines.

### 5.3 Privacy Disputes and Appeals

If a customer disagrees with a privacy decision:

1. Reply to the privacy decision notice or submit an appeal through the designated channel.
2. Include the request identifier.
3. State the specific decision being challenged.
4. Provide additional information or documentation.
5. The Privacy & Security Office conducts secondary review.
6. The Company issues an outcome within a reasonable period and within any mandatory legal deadline.

### 5.4 External Regulatory Complaints

Where applicable, customers may have the right to complain to their local supervisory authority, data-protection regulator, consumer-protection agency, or other competent authority.

The appropriate authority depends on the customer's location, the Company's establishment, and the applicable law.

### 5.5 Policy Changes

The Company may update this policy to reflect:

- New services;
- Security improvements;
- Changes in technology;
- Regulatory requirements;
- Changes in data processing arrangements.

Material changes may be communicated through the website, application, email, account notifications, or another legally appropriate method.

The policy's **Version** and **Effective Date** identify the current operational version.

### 5.6 Contact and Escalation Records

Customers should retain:

- Request confirmation numbers;
- Privacy ticket IDs;
- Security incident IDs;
- Relevant dates and times;
- Copies of Company notices.

These identifiers help the Company locate the correct record and reduce processing delays.
